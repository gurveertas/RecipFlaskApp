What this tool is

An AI content pipeline that produces verified, cited, QA-gated blog articles for rededucation.com. The model never invents facts: every article is grounded in sources retrieved live, every statistic must trace to a source, and nothing reaches WordPress until automated QA passes. Article Studio researches only the trusted-domain allowlist and also requires the REACS AI-search-visibility standard; Quick draft uses the open web only when nothing on that list covers the topic.

There are two ways to generate:

  • Blog Generator (the home page) — a quick draft on any topic. Nothing is saved to the database; a finished draft is held for one hour so you can rewrite a section, proofread it or send it to WordPress. Good for one-offs and topics outside the vendor catalogue.
  • Article Studio — the governed 6-step flow (Vendor → Topic → Brief → Article → Preview → Publish). Everything is saved, scored, and quality-gated. This is the main path.
Blog Generator (quick draft)
  • Topic — free text, anything. In Live mode it drives the research: the trusted domains first, and the open web only when nothing on that list ranks for the topic, so an IT subject is still researched from the curated sources. Naming the vendor helps ("Palo Alto Panorama management" beats "centralised management").
  • Keywords (optional, up to 8) — press Enter/comma after each. Every keyword must appear verbatim in the draft; the QA harness checks this mechanically.
  • Region (optional) — localises salary/market context where sources support it.
  • Demo vs Live — Demo uses bundled sample sources and a deterministic writer (free, instant, no keys), so its topic is fixed to the sample. Live searches the web, verifies claims, and writes with the LLM.
  • Results stream step-by-step; download as .md/.json or copy. Drafts are not saved — use Studio for anything you want to keep.
  • Its own QA standard. A Quick draft goes through the same Reliability QA for grounding, citations, originality, spelling and attribution, with a 600-word floor and a 900–2,100-word target instead of Studio's 1,300 and 1,500–2,100. A Red Education course link is only asked for when the topic is one of ours. See The QA checks.
  • The finished draft is held for one hour so the tools under it work: Rewrite section (pick an H2, optionally add an instruction; QA re-runs on the result) and Proofread (advisory wording suggestions, which you apply yourself). Both are offered whatever the QA verdict. After the hour, generate it again.
  • Send to WordPress appears only when Reliability QA passes. It creates a draft post, never a live one. A Quick draft has no vendor and no reviewing instructor, so no category and no “Technically reviewed by” line are set — add a category in WordPress before publishing. Sending the same draft twice is refused, because it would create a duplicate.
Article Studio — the 6 steps

Step 1 · Vendor

  • Cards are sorted by priority (P1 highest). Priority is set in Manage vendors and does two things: it enables topic generation and it weights topic scoring (higher-priority vendors score higher).
  • Each card separates two things. live on rededucation.com — how many published pages already cover that vendor, from the site scrape; the quickest read on where coverage exists and where it doesn't (a post covering two vendors counts for both). The line under the vendor list says how fresh that count is — see Where the numbers come from. Then in this tool, split into mutually exclusive stages that add up to the total: in brief (never written), needs fixes (written, QA or REACS holding it), ready (both gates passed, not sent), awaiting publish (a draft is sitting in WordPress), live (someone has published it).
  • The footer line is about brief quality, not article readiness: Open opportunity means the vendor has a priority and knowledge-base data but no coverage on the site yet.
  • A Research only card has no commercial priority. Its source domain and historical coverage stay available for factual research, but topic generation, new briefs, article writing and WordPress pushes are blocked server-side. Set a priority only for a current commercial vendor.
  • Purge all topics & articles deletes every topic idea and every article in this tool, across all vendors, after a confirmation showing both counts. It cannot be undone. Vendors, certifications, courses and instructors are kept, and drafts already in WordPress are not touched.

Step 2 · Topic

  • Count (5/10/15) — how many ideas to generate. Theme (optional) — steer the batch, e.g. "zero trust".
  • What happens on Generate: the LLM proposes ideas and estimates raw dimensions; a deterministic formula (commercial 40% / SEO 25% / AI-visibility 20% / vendor-alignment 15%, minus difficulty) decides. The model never decides.
  • Difficulty is measured, not guessed: each keyword's live Google top-10 is analysed (authority domains, exact-title matches) and replaces the LLM's estimate before scoring. Costs ~1 Serper credit per idea.
  • Score badge: ≥65 → create (approved), ≥45 → parked, below → rejected. You can override by clicking "Approve & create brief" on a parked topic.
  • gap / overlap lane: every idea is checked against real published rededucation.com posts. gap = nothing similar exists (good). overlap = a close match already ranks — linked so you can judge whether to duplicate.
  • Long focus keyword warning: a keyword over ~40 characters or 5 words can't fit an SEO title (which caps at 60), so Rank Math scores the page badly no matter how good the article is. It's a warning, not a block — but it's much cheaper to reword the keyword here than after it has propagated into the brief, title, slug and SEO title.
  • Create brief runs live research for that topic and drafts the brief. It refuses in two distinct cases, and the message says which: no usable sources at all (the search is restricted to this vendor's allowlisted domains, so a very specific topic can match nothing), or sources that don't mention the subject — writing then would produce a confident article about something else. If a reputable missing source appeared in the open-web comparison search, review it in Allowlist gaps before trying again.
  • Ideas that already have work show how far they got, using the same stages as the vendor card: brief (created, not written yet), needs fixes or ready (with word count, QA and REACS scores), awaiting publish (a draft is sitting in WordPress, with a link to open it) and live (the publish date and a link to the post). Their button becomes View brief or View article: each idea holds one article, so it can't be briefed twice.
  • Remove takes an unwanted idea off the vendor's list after a confirmation. An idea that already has a brief or article is refused rather than deleted along with that work.

Step 3 · Brief

  • The brief is the editorial contract the writer must follow. Every field is editable — edit here and Save brief, not after writing.
  • Title — the article title, up to 120 characters. The hint underneath says which of Rank Math's four title checks the saved title fails, if any.
  • Keyword — the SEO target. Checked as an exact substring in the final article, and used as the search query for research. Changing it changes what gets retrieved.
  • Outline — one H2 heading per line, in order. The article will follow it.
  • Key points — one per line; specific claims/angles the article must make.
  • CTA intent — a steer for the closing call-to-action (free text). The final CTA must still contain one of the approved brand phrases character-for-character; this field shapes the sentence around it.
  • Suggested internal links — real, currently-published rededucation.com posts and course pages matched to this brief. "+ Add" makes them required: QA warns if the writer drops one.
  • Add your own link — any http(s) URL (a course page, a landing page). Same required treatment.
  • Write article — runs fresh research and injects the vendor's certifications and courses as separate authoritative lists, plus an approved menu of real internal pages to link. The same entity contract reaches the brief, full write, targeted fixes and section rewrites. It forbids treating a course as a credential, unsupported prerequisites/renames/logistics, stale promotions, unattributed house advice and unexplained cross-vendor recommendations. Writing gets up to 3 attempts; failed QA checks feed targeted fixes into each retry. Takes 1–3 minutes.

Step 4 · Article

  • The verdict banner is the headline: Publish candidate needs BOTH QA and REACS to pass; Blocked names which gate(s) failed. WARNs never block — they're reviewer attention items.
  • Both gates now carry a 0–100 score shown side by side, on the same weighting so they read on comparable terms (integrity/mandatory 60, advisory 40; a WARN takes half credit). The QA score grades quality — it does not gate: qa_passed is still "nothing failed". A blocked draft never shows a green score, because a single failure among many checks still scores high, and green next to a blocked verdict would contradict it.
  • The Reliability QA and REACS panels list every check, filterable by "Needs attention" / "Passing" / "All". REACS is a second, separate gate — see below — with its own score and pass/fail per pillar.
  • Fix selected — tick the specific checks you want fixed and leave the rest; Blocking, All and Clear set the ticks in one click. Blocking issues (FAIL / mandatory) start ticked; WARNs and advisories don't, since those are often a deliberate call. Grounded in the article's own sources. Use this first.
    • Mechanical problems — Australian spelling, vendor and acronym casing, a paragraph that ended up inside a heading — are corrected directly, with no AI call.
    • The rest goes to the AI as numbered paragraphs, and it returns a list of edits. A paragraph it doesn't name is not rewritten at all, and any edit that breaks a check which was passing is dropped.
    • Normally 1 AI call, up to 3 when a fix and a regression sit in the same sentences. If the result would leave the article unpublishable, the whole attempt is discarded and the article stays as it was.
    • A check that has already resisted a fix is labelled did not clear last time (or how many attempts are running), so you can see that before spending another call on it.
  • Re-score — re-runs both gates against the article exactly as it stands. No AI call and no search credits: it reads the article's own saved sources. Use it after a manual edit, or when a finding quotes text that is no longer in the article. REACS always re-runs; if a source page can't be read again, the QA half is skipped and the message says so, rather than leaving an old score looking current.
  • Proofread the body — one AI read for wording problems the deterministic checks can't judge. Advisory: it never blocks. Each suggestion quotes the exact text it would change; tick the ones you want and apply them. Applying is an exact text swap with no AI call, so nothing else in the sentence moves, and both gates are re-scored. A suggestion whose text has changed since is skipped and marked — proofread again to refresh it.
  • Edit the article yourself — the escape hatch for when the automated fixes can't do it. Opens the body markdown, the title, both meta fields and the FAQ for direct editing. What you type is saved exactly as typed, with no AI call and no search credits, and both gates are re-scored immediately, so the panels always describe the text that is actually there. Nothing is refused at the form: an edit that breaks a check shows up as that check failing. Keep each [S#] marker on the sentence it supports, and if a draft is already in WordPress, remember this changes only the copy in this tool.
  • An instructor byline ("Technically reviewed by …") is auto-assigned on first write, but only to an instructor certified for this article's vendor. Where several qualify the least-used is picked, so bylines rotate. A vendor with no certified instructor falls back to the full roster, rotated the same way, so every article carries a byline. The credential line always lists the vendors that instructor genuinely holds. This is not a gate: nothing blocks on it.
  • References — the numbered [S#] sources the article cites. These are the actual pages the facts came from.
  • Rewrite one section — pick an H2, optionally give an instruction ("add the exam cost", "link to <url>"). One LLM call, ~10s, citations preserved, full QA + REACS re-run. For edits no check has flagged.
  • Rewrite whole article — full research + write + QA cycle. Use when the article is structurally wrong, not for small fixes.
  • Download PDF — available at any stage, findings or not: the article in rededucation.com's formatting (as on the Preview step, with every FAQ answer expanded), the numbered references on a new page, and a Quality report with both verdicts and scores, every QA check and REACS pillar with its detail, and the article's parameters.
  • Continue to Preview appears only when QA and REACS both pass. An article written before REACS existed shows "not yet scored" rather than a false failure — any rewrite (even a single section) computes it.

Step 5 · Preview

  • The article rendered in rededucation.com's own article formatting — type sizes, colours, table styling and the FAQ accordion were measured from live published posts, not approximated. This is the last point where a formatting problem is cheap to fix.
  • Desktop / Tablet / Mobile narrow the text column exactly as the live one does, so a wide comparison table that overflows on a phone overflows here too.
  • The FAQ renders as the site's accordion, identified from the article's stored FAQ data rather than by guessing at headings. If a draft has no FAQ section the page says so instead of inventing one.
  • The [S#] citation markers are not shown here, because they are not published either — they exist so QA can verify every claim traces to a source. The Article step still shows them.
  • What it can't show: site header, footer, sidebar, related-posts strip and the hero image — those come from the WordPress theme at publish time.

Step 6 · Publish

  • Creates a draft in WordPress. It never publishes live — the status is hard-coded and re-checked before sending, so going live stays a human action in WordPress.
  • QA and REACS are re-run immediately before the push. A green verdict stored under older rules cannot bypass a control added later, and a draft whose saved sources can no longer be rebuilt is stopped for review.
  • What will be sent lists every field with its status. Title, slug, body, excerpt, SEO title/description, author and category are filled automatically. Featured image is attached by an editor by hand. Tags are not sent.
  • Posts are authored by a single configured WordPress account, and the category comes from the vendor — a direct lookup, not an AI guess.
  • Exact payload (dry run) shows the complete request body without contacting WordPress, including the JSON-LD schema (Article + FAQPage) and the inline table styling that makes generated tables match hand-written posts.
  • After pushing, the card becomes a receipt with Edit in WordPress. That opens the wp-admin editor, not a public URL: a draft has no public address — visiting one logged out returns the site's 404.
  • Pushing twice is blocked while the draft exists (it would duplicate, not update). Delete the draft in WordPress and the article becomes pushable again.
The QA checks — what each one means

FAIL blocks publishing and triggers a rewrite attempt. WARN informs — the article still passes.

The "Can fail?" column is also what the QA score weighs: the checks that can fail are the integrity tier (60 points), the warn-only ones are advisory (40). Several checks are conditional — keyword_coverage only runs with keywords, entity_coverage only with vendor entities, serp_competitiveness only with a live SERP snapshot — so the score is calculated over the checks that actually ran, never a fixed count.

REACS — the AI Content Standard

A second, independent gate alongside QA above — same idea (deterministic checks, never scored by the model), different question. QA asks "is this draft internally sound?" (grounded, cited, on-brief). REACS asks "does this meet Red Education's AI-search-visibility content standard?" The two panels can disagree — an article can pass QA cleanly and still fail REACS, or vice versa; publishing needs both. Score = mandatory pillars × 60% + advisory pillars × 40%, rounded. Any single mandatory failure blocks REACS regardless of score. Pass requires zero mandatory failures and a score of at least 90. Six mandatory pillars and six advisory — the instructor-byline pillar was taken off the gate by decision, so a missing byline no longer blocks a publish.

comparison_tableMandatoryAt least one markdown table (courses, comparisons, exam domains).
faq_depthMandatoryAt least 8 FAQs with written answers (target 10). Answers are counted, not questions — an empty accordion body does not count.

freshness_stampMandatoryA visible "Last updated:" line carrying a full date ("19 August 2026"). A month and year alone fails. Every accepted full write, targeted fix, section rewrite or proofreading edit refreshes this line mechanically to the real edit date.

Manage vendors (Vendor Centre)
  • Priority (P1–P5) — the business and partnership lever: gates topic generation, brief creation, article writing and WordPress pushes, and weights topic scoring. Unset = research-only; sources and historical coverage remain, but no new commercial content can move through the pipeline.
  • Products — the vendor's product line with lifecycle status; context for the writer.
  • Certifications — current or retired. Marking a cert retired matters: it becomes a QA tripwire, warning any article that treats it as current. Retirement year, replacement cert, and custom warning notes are all used. A row that is really a training course (its only course has the same name and no exam version is recorded) is badged course, not a credential: it isn't counted as a certification on the vendor card, isn't given to the writer as a credential, and the Topic Engine won't propose certification guides about it. Give it a real credential name and exam version to change that.
  • Courses — must belong to a certification and have a URL. Fed to the writer as context, entity-coverage targets, and real internal-link candidates.
Allowlist gaps

This is the human review queue for domains that rank for our live research queries but are not yet trusted sources. Retrieval runs an open-web comparison search alongside its normal scoped search, then applies the same allowlist to both. A domain shown here was discarded before its page was fetched and has not supplied facts, citations, or text to an article.

Reading the queue

  • Awaiting a decision contains new or repeatedly seen domains, ordered by the number of sightings and then recency.
  • Seen is how many search results have used that domain. Treat frequency as a reason to inspect it, not as a trust or quality score.
  • Latest result links to the most recently seen page, and Found for shows the query that produced it. Review both the page and the wider domain before approving: a relevant title can still come from an exam-dump farm, a competitor, or a different subject sharing the same acronym.

Classifying a source

  • Vendor — an official vendor-owned domain. Attach it to the matching vendor so vendor-scoped research and the vendor-grounding check can recognise it. Only this category accepts a vendor assignment.
  • Authority — a government, regulator, standards body, or similarly authoritative independent source.
  • Press — established editorial, trade-publication, or news coverage.
  • Data — a credible research or statistics source that does not belong in the jobs-only bucket.
  • Jobs — salary and vacancy aggregators. These are included only when the topic has salary, career, hiring, or related intent because broad job sites can produce misleading acronym matches.
  • Tier 1 is for authoritative first-party or primary sources; Tier 2 is for established secondary sources. A high Seen count is never a reason to promote a source to Tier 1.

Making a decision

  • Approve adds the domain to the trusted source allowlist with the selected category, tier, and vendor assignment. The queue entry is removed, and future research can retrieve and cite pages from that domain. Existing briefs and articles are unchanged; rerun Brief or Write when you need fresh research.
  • Ignore keeps the domain blocked and moves it out of the active queue. It does not add the domain to research and does not delete its sighting history.
  • Ignored domains is the reversible holding list. Restore returns a domain to Awaiting a decision; it still remains blocked until separately approved.
  • Approval trusts the domain, not just the one result shown. Approve only when pages across that domain are suitable for a published reference list.
Where the numbers come from

Two figures on the Vendor step are read from outside this tool, so it is worth knowing how current they are.

  • “live on rededucation.com” — the big number on each vendor card. It comes from a scrape of the site's own sitemap, not from a live query, so it is only as fresh as the last run. The line under the heading says when that was (“Site coverage last checked 2 days ago”). It refreshes automatically overnight; if you have just published something and want the count now, ask IT to run scrape_published_posts.py.
  • “live” vs “awaiting publish” — once an article is pushed, this tool only ever creates a draft. It then checks back with WordPress during the working day and marks the article live when someone has actually published it. Until then it reads awaiting publish, which is your reminder that a draft is sitting in WordPress waiting for a human.

Both checks are read-only: nothing here publishes a post, changes one, or edits the live site.

Usage & costs
  • The Usage chip (every header, top right) shows Serper credits left and Gemini calls today against the 500/day free cap. Click it for 7-day totals and a per-feature spend split.
  • Serper credits left is an estimate. Serper has no balance API, so the tool counts what this server spends. Searches made anywhere else — a script on a laptop, the Serper playground — are invisible to it. After a top-up, a new key or any outside searching, paste the real number from serper.dev into Reconcile in that window; everything spent afterwards counts down from it.
  • Repeat searches are free for 6 hours. The same query within that window reuses the earlier result and spends nothing; a failed search is retried after 10 minutes. The cache lives in memory, so a restart or deploy clears it.
  • Rough costs: topic batch ≈ 1 Serper credit per new keyword + 1 AI call · create brief ≈ 4–6 credits (1 per 8 trusted domains searched, plus 1 open-web comparison search) + 1 AI call · write or rewrite whole article ≈ the same research again, 1 credit for the keyword's live Google results, and 1–3 AI calls · Fix selected ≈ 1–3 AI calls · Rewrite one section or Proofread ≈ 1 AI call. Fixes and section rewrites also read the keyword's live results, which is free if they were fetched in the last 6 hours. Re-score, applying proofread suggestions, Preview and the PDF cost nothing.
  • Balances on serper.dev and AI Studio are ground truth; the in-app meter attributes spend per feature.
Tips
  • A blocked article isn't wasted — read the failed check's detail; it names the exact problem (the copied phrase, the missing keyword, the unsourced figure). Try Fix selected on just that check before reaching for a full rewrite.
  • Genuinely niche topics can stay blocked on length/grounding after 3 attempts — that's the system refusing to pad or invent. Pick a topic with more source coverage, or accept and edit manually.
  • REACS failing on direct_answer_first or single_search_intent almost always means the opening paragraph, the title or the SEO title doesn't literally contain the keyword. A section rewrite won't fix this (it only touches one H2). Fix selected can edit the opening paragraph and the SEO title; if the article title itself lacks the keyword, correct it in the Title field on the Brief step, then Re-score.
  • A finding that quotes a sentence no longer in the article means the report predates an edit — press Re-score before acting on it.
  • When a check is labelled did not clear last time, stop paying for another attempt at it: open Edit the article yourself and correct the sentence directly. A wrong figure or a clumsy line is usually faster to fix than to describe to a model.
  • Use the ◑ button (any header) to switch light/dark theme.
  • Keywords beat titles for research: the brief's keyword is the search query, so keep it the canonical vendor-named phrase.
